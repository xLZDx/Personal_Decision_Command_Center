01

What was proposed

Two things in one document. The first is the transport: replace the user-account connector built on TDLib, living on an always-on Utility Host, with a Telegram bot connected to your own account through the Business / connected-bot API. By default — one-to-one private conversations only, no groups. This removes TDLib, the session file, the local spool, the Cloudflare Tunnel and the Content Gateway.

The second is the consent model: four access classes (TG_AI_DENY_DEFAULT, TG_AI_ALLOW_DIRECT_INTERACTION, TG_AI_ALLOW_CHAT_SCOPE, TG_AI_ALLOW_MESSAGE_SCOPE), an AIConsentGrant object and amendments to INV-03A / 04A / 05A / 26A, after which part of the Telegram content would be allowed to reach the AI.

The halves are separated deliberately. They are independent: the bot transport does not require the consent model, and the consent model does not require the bot. Merged into a single document, they read as one package — and accepting the transport would have dragged along a change to the invariants that never got a discussion of its own.
02

The rejection and why it did not hold

How the reviewer reasoned

The Content Licensing Terms contain a consent exception, and that option had already been considered and rejected in this very repository on 10 September: docs/architecture/EXTERNAL_ASSUMPTIONS.md, lines 81–95. The exception requires "explicit, informed, affirmative and continued consent" from all relevant users — that is, from the correspondents — is tied to a specific chat and is non-transferable. For ordinary two-party conversations this is unattainable. The implementer recommended admitting the narrowest class; GPT-PM refused even that one.

That reasoning rested on a premise the text does not support

The operator objected. Instead of repeating the verdict, the primary sources were re-read — and the premise that "the AI regime for Bot API data is set by the Content Licensing Terms" was not confirmed. The very document where the prohibition lives says, verbatim:

"Telegram permits access to data required to launch and operate a legitimate third-party Telegram Client, Telegram Bot, or Telegram Mini App" — subject to compliance with the corresponding Terms of Service.

That is, a legitimate bot is redirected to the Bot Platform Developer Terms. This sentence was recorded in §B as far back as 10 September, but was read as permission for access; that it also redirects the regime of use went unnoticed. Whether the ban on AI use reaches data lawfully obtained by a bot does not follow in either direction from the text. That is UNKNOWN, not a fact.

Telegram takes the disclosure on itself

Privacy Policy §6.5, verbatim: "If you message a Business user who connected a chatbot to their account, depending on their settings, you may be effectively communicating with a bot, and it will receive basic data about you as detailed in section 6.3 above." This is a term every Telegram user accepts. The objection about covert interception without the other party's knowledge is thereby removed.

There is, however, no badge in the chat. Neither the Privacy Policy, nor core.telegram.org/api/business, nor /api/bots/connected-business-bots describes any marker visible to the correspondent; an industry source claims there is none at all. This does not affect the argument — disclosure in a policy is a stronger instrument than an interface element — but no claim about a badge can be made.
02a

The real constraint is not where either side was looking for it

Bot Developer Terms §5.4(iv)

"never disclose message contents, files or other data you obtained or processed through or in connection with Telegram Business to third parties (including third-party APIs) without the user's authorization"

Sending Telegram content to an external model is exactly a "disclosure to a third-party API". "The user's authorization" is required. Whose exactly — the text does not settle. In the same §5.4, item (v) says "business account owner" in so many words, so the document is capable of distinguishing the owner from the correspondent — and in (iv) that phrase is absent. The ambiguity is genuine; no reading can be proved from the text.

Bot Developer Terms §4.3, paragraph 2 — an explicit permission

"you are free to use data submitted directly and voluntarily to your TPA by users, provided that you clearly inform them of the data's intended use and they give their individual, explicit, active and revocable consent"

The standard is different and attainable: "individual, explicit, active and revocable" instead of "continued consent from all relevant users". It requires clear information about the purpose — that is, precisely what option B in section 10 does.

And paragraph 1 of the same §4.3 prohibits collection aimed at creating datasets, models and AI products. Personal Decision OS creates nothing and trains nothing — it consumes someone else's model. That is a different act from the prohibited one.

The conclusion I am obliged to state honestly

The Telegram AI question is reopened and unresolved. It is neither "prohibited" nor "permitted". The prohibition the reviewer found is a finding to be verified, not a veto; verification showed that the basis does not hold. The decision is the operator's; the options are in section 10.

04

The claim about ADR-011 is wrong

§17 of the proposal asserts that once TDLib is removed, the pre-defined ADR-011 fallback no longer requires a host. core/adr/ADR-011-queue-consumer-runtime.md, lines 65–73, says exactly the opposite:

"…the queue consumer switches to Cloudflare's documented HTTP pull consumer running on the already-required connector host (same host as the Telegram TDLib connector)…"

"…nightly D1 export/compress/encrypt/upload-to-R2 runs on the connector host (host/backup-agent/), never inside a Worker"

Removing the Telegram connector does not remove the host — it removes one of its two jobs. The second — the R8 pull-consumer fallback and the nightly backup — remains. The Utility Host stays. GPT-PM confirmed this in a separate item of the ruling.

This also changes the claimed scale of the simplification. TDLib, the session, the spool, the tunnel and the gateway go — that is a real gain. The host does not go.

06

A regression in the reviewer's own verdict

In round 1 GPT-PM proposed replacing INV-30 with a wording carrying a caveat — "encrypted at rest where platform capability permits". That caveat is unavailable.

§4.4(a) is unconditional. On D1 under HARD_ZERO there is no key held by the operator on the platform side. So the wording "where the platform permits" would evaluate to "D1 gives nothing, therefore nothing is required" — exactly the silent downgrade that core/adr/ADR-007:54 already forbids.

Raised in the second round. GPT-PM accepted the correction and removed the caveat. This also once more reduces the claimed simplification: central storage brings back the obligation to encrypt with separate key storage — conceptually the same machinery the proposal was removing along with the ECDH envelope, only reassigned.

07

New invariant texts — as ruled, but not adopted

INV-30A — Telegram stored-data encryption

Any Telegram user data retained by the service MUST be encrypted at rest before persistence, and the encryption key MUST be stored separately from the encrypted data. No plaintext Telegram source content may be persisted in D1, R2 backups, logs, metrics, queues, CI fixtures, or equivalent durable stores. Decryption is permitted only after an authenticated and authorized evidence request. Responses carrying source content are no-store and must never enter service-worker caches.

INV-14A — Telegram minimization, purpose limitation and deletion

Telegram user data may be retained centrally only to the extent essential to operate an explicitly documented Personal Decision OS service function. The system MUST NOT collect, store, aggregate or process Telegram data beyond that operational necessity. Retained Telegram user data MUST be deleted without undue delay when deletion is requested where applicable, when it is no longer necessary for the service, and when the Telegram TPA ceases operation except where an expressly valid retention agreement or legal obligation permits otherwise. Detailed TTLs, edit/delete propagation, disconnect behavior, tombstones and backup-erasure procedures are defined in the source-aware retention policy and may not weaken this invariant.

The layering is deliberate: INV-14A is the non-reducible contractual floor, DATA_RETENTION_POLICY.md holds the operational detail. Wordings that present central Telegram storage as a product preference are to be removed: in the bot model minimization and deletion are contractual obligations.

08

Three preconditions before adoption

1 · An empirical check on your account without Premium

The whole transport stands on this fact, and Telegram's legal and product documents diverge. The check costs a minute and settles the question better than any further reading.

  • Confirm that Settings → Chat Automation exists without Premium.
  • Connect a test bot with Secretary Mode enabled in BotFather.
  • Confirm that a BusinessConnection arrives and that getMe returns can_connect_to_business.
  • Pass one ordinary private message in each direction through the documented Bot API path.
  • Record the client, platform, version and date.

An important distinction: the check proves technical availability for your account. It does not prove which Telegram wording is legally binding. The §6.5 / §5.4 divergence remains an accepted unresolved documentation contradiction — it cannot be silently "closed" by a successful test.

2 · Where the key lives — an open design decision

GPT-PM deliberately did not pick an option: this is a mandatory SEC/PRIV decision before adoption, not an improvisation inside G4. Three candidates, each with its own price:

  • Utility Host — the availability of Telegram evidence again depends on an always-on host.
  • The key in the PWA — complicates webhook reception, work from several devices, recovery and backups.
  • Envelope encryption, the KEK in a secret store separate from D1 — technically simpler, but must not be chosen without a threat-model review.

The amendment cannot be adopted until custody, recovery, rotation and backup semantics have an independently reviewed design.

3 · A §B snapshot of the Bot API documents

docs/architecture/EXTERNAL_ASSUMPTIONS.md §B today contains not a single line about the Bot API — only the API Terms and Content Licensing are recorded there, with hashes from 10 September. The amendment rests entirely on facts the file does not record. A snapshot of the same shape is needed: URL, retrieval date, SHA-256, verbatim quotes — including the contradictory Privacy Policy §6.5 and Bot ToS §5.4.

What carries over into the G4 acceptance criteria

  • The bot needs Secretary Mode enabled in BotFather; getMe exposes can_connect_to_business.
  • Marking messages as read is not automatic: it is optional and limited by the can_read_messages right. This removes the API Terms §1.4 concern for a receive-only bot.
  • Replying and marking as read are limited to chats active in the last 24 hours.
  • Telegram keeps undelivered updates for no more than 24 hours — which means D1 becomes the long-term record immediately upon reception.
  • Two documentation pages are dead: telegram.org/faq_business is an empty shell, and core.telegram.org/api/bots/business returns "Page not found".
PR #4 was put on HOLD by the same verdict: among the nine manifest proposals there is G4, whose scope will change substantially if the amendment is adopted.
